# ADR 0007: AI-assisted work: human credited, AI tagged

Status: Accepted · 2026-10-14 · Deciders: Joseph Peterson

## Context

In 2026, much AI-written code is committed under a human's name. Detecting bots by author name misses it. What git records:

- Claude Code adds `Co-Authored-By: <model name> <noreply@anthropic.com>`. The name varies, so match the email.
- Aider adds `Co-authored-by: aider (<model>) <aider@aider.chat>`.
- GitHub's Copilot coding agent is the commit author, with the human as co-author.
- Cursor's trailer string could not be verified.
- Editor completions leave no trace at all.

## Decision

- **Credit the human.** When an agent is the author and a human is a co-author, the human is credited.
- **Tag the commit** `ai_assisted` when a trailer or author matches the policy's pattern list. The list is versioned (`ai_assisted@N`) because conventions change.
- **Compare cohorts**, AI-assisted against other changes, in team and code-area views. A person's own AI-assisted share is shown only to that person.
- **Agents are never ranked rows.** Name-matched bots (dependency updaters and the like) keep their separate table.

## Alternatives considered

- **Agents as contributors**, with their own rows and split credit. Any split rule is arbitrary and would have to be defended.
- **Ignoring AI signals** (the 2026-10-07 plan). AI-assisted work becomes invisible.

Prior art: git-ai records line-level AI attribution in git notes, but only for commits made after it is installed.

## Consequences

- The impact of AI tools becomes visible, as a lower bound.
- The pattern list needs maintenance as tools change their trailers.

## Door

Two-way.

## Revisit if

A standard AI-attribution trailer emerges, or git-ai style notes become common enough to read alongside trailers.

## Amendment: the shares that leave the machine

Status: Proposed · 2026-10-15 · Deciders: Joseph Peterson · Amends "A person's own AI-assisted share is shown only to that person" for the MCP server's answers ([mcp.md](../spec/mcp.md))

### Context

The MCP server sent a repository's or a group's AI-assisted share only when at least three people landed in its window, so that no share it sent was close to one person's own. It decided one scope at a time. Shares add up over the scopes, since a group's landings are its repositories' and the whole run's are every repository's, so shares sent side by side can be subtracted. The review of 2026-10-15 (#68) found it in the first answer an agent asks for: a group of `team` (four people, 1 of 12 landings AI-assisted) and `solo` (one person, 3 of 4) showed the group at 4 of 16 and `team` at 1 of 12, and (4 − 1) of (16 − 12) is the solo person's own 3 of 4. The refusal for `solo` pointed at the group's share as well. The review of the fix found two more ways out: `explain` of surviving@1 about `solo` sent its share as `ai_assisted_share`, and one person who committed from three addresses no `.mailmap` merged counted as three people.

### Decision

- **The threshold stays three.** A share leaves the machine only when at least three people are credited with a non-merge commit of a landing in its 365-day window (`app.AIShareMinPeople`). Three is the minimum frequency rule of disclosure control: one of three people who takes away their own landings still sees two people's together.
- **People, not addresses, and the count errs low.** A canonical address after `.mailmap` is not a person: one developer commits from a work address, GitHub's noreply address and a laptop's made-up one, and three of those would pass the threshold alone. The addresses of one block count as one person (`identity.Blocks` over the whole run): those the identities rules link (`identity.Candidates`, at every confidence, hand-offs included), those of every hand-off chain however long, and every two that share any name seen with them (a default one such as John Doe included), a local part or a GitHub account, without the vetoes those rules apply. The rules lean to precision because they suggest merges; the floor leans to recall, as record linkage's blocking step does (Christen, *Data Matching*, Springer 2012, ch. 4), since the end-to-end check of the merged fix found one person under three spellings of a name (Sam Solo, S. Solo, Samuel Solo), on addresses sharing a local part, counted as three. The count never merges anyone on a page, which only `.mailmap` does; it only decides what leaves. A wrong link only lowers a count, so it never lets out a share, or a part of one that no other shown share covers, of fewer than three people; only a missed link can (known limits, addresses with nothing in common). It need not make fewer shares leave: with a lower count, secondary suppression can show a sibling the other count withheld, and withhold one it showed (known limits, one run and two versions). An empty author address is a missing fact, not a person: beside other people it may be any of them and adds no one, and alone it is one person, so a count of none always means no person's landings. That holds while the run takes the empty address for a person's: identity decides it once per run, by the name most often seen with it, and if that name is a bot's, every empty-email landing is a bot's, which leaves its AI tag uncounted rather than disclosed (#74).
- **Secondary (complementary) suppression.** Bottom-up over the tree of scopes (the whole run, its groups, their repositories, and the repositories named on their own): when the withheld children of a scope leave a part of it that covers one or two people, more children are withheld, the smallest first (fewest people, then kind and key), until the part covers three or more. When no child can do that, the scope's own share is withheld; only the whole run can come to that, when every child it still shows is a group whose withheld repositories credit no one: it has none, or they hold only bots' landings. Every residual, a shown share less the shown shares below it, then covers no one or at least three people, at every level and across levels. `internal/app/aishare.go` holds the argument.
- **One decision per run, over every copy of a share.** `app.AIShares` is a pure function of a run's facts, which `app.RunNames` computes with the vocabulary every call reads, so every tool of one version reads the same decision and no two of its answers about a run disagree or can be subtracted: `overview` and `explain` hold scopes' shares, `person` holds only the user's own on their own page, and `changes` and `highlights` hold none. A share has more than one carrier: surviving@1's population makeup copies its scope's share as `ai_assisted_share`, and runs already stored keep that copy. `report.AIShareIn` is the one place that names every value holding a share, and the server withholds each copy where it withholds the share. A withheld share leaves with neither its `num` nor its `den`, nor any copy. The `ai-few-people` caveat and `explain`'s refusal say that a share is withheld, and point at no share it could be worked out from. Since 2026-10-19 ([ADR 0015](0015-shares-are-decided-against-a-record-of-what-left.md)) the decision that leaves is this one only for a run the server has not answered about before: it is recorded before its first share leaves, every later answer about the run reads the record, and a new run's decision is checked against the shares recorded for every window that overlaps its own.
- **The whole run's share** is decided on only where an answer can show it, the overview of every repository, which a run has when it was scanned with `--scope all` or has no groups (`report.WholeRun`).
- **The local report is unchanged.** It shows every share; whether it should apply the same rule is an open decision for the owner ([product.md's open decision 7](../product.md#open-decisions)).

### Prior art

- Statistical agencies publish nested tables this way: a primary rule, such as a minimum frequency, and secondary cell suppression, solved over hierarchical tables by Statistics Netherlands' τ-ARGUS (its modular method) and R's sdcTable (Hundepool et al., *Statistical Disclosure Control*, Wiley 2012, ch. 4).
- Developer analytics apply the primary rule as a minimum group size: GitHub's Copilot metrics API answers for a team only on days when it had five or more members with an active Copilot licence, and Microsoft Viva Insights shows no group smaller than its minimum group size, five by default and at the least.

### Alternatives considered

- **The threshold per scope alone**, the rule this amends: it leaks by subtraction, as above.
- **Withholding the parent of every withheld child**: simple and safe, but one person's repository would cost its group's share and the run's.
- **Deciding per answer**: an answer about a group and another about one of its repositories could each be safe alone and leak together.
- **Noise or rounding** (differential privacy, controlled rounding): every share stays, but its numbers would no longer be the report's, which every answer promises ([mcp.md](../spec/mcp.md)), and noise that hides one person among three swamps the share.
- **A higher threshold**, five as GitHub and Viva Insights use: on its own it does nothing against subtraction. It is a separate choice, which this amendment leaves at three.
- **Dropping surviving@1's copy of the share** from the metric: one carrier fewer for new runs, but runs already stored keep it, so the server must withhold it anyway. It stays a cleanup for a later metric change, which the server's rule does not depend on.
- **Counting people by display name**, as the `unmerged-identities` caveat groups them: it links fewer addresses than the identities rules do (none by local part, by GitHub account or by hand-off), so it counts higher, which is the direction that leaks.

### Consequences

- A share of three or more people can be withheld to protect a smaller one (`team` above). A scope withholds at most one more child for it, since a shown child's residual covers no one or at least three people, and the caveat names every withheld scope without saying why each is withheld.
- Counting people by block withholds the share of three people of whom a block joins two wrongly, such as two who share a name, a default one such as John Doe included, or a role local part such as `root` on two machines. It costs every call one survey of the run's identities, of which the blocks add about 1%: 8.3 ms on the run of Homebrew/brew and three of its taps (616 addresses), and 73 ms on a synthetic run of 30 repositories, 90,000 commits and 600,000 blame records (Apple M4 Max, Go 1.27.2; 122 ms on another generator). On brew's 365-day window, 198 human addresses, the identities rules count 184 people and blocks 172; no decision changes, and of random three-person teams drawn from the 184, blocks count 0.3% below three.
- Known limits, each with what would close it:
  - **Two runs**, closed 2026-10-19 wherever the subtraction is exact ([ADR 0015](0015-shares-are-decided-against-a-record-of-what-left.md), #85). The MCP server records each run's decision before its first share leaves, and decides a new run against every share recorded for a window that overlaps its own, whatever its `--now`. A repository that carries the same counts in both runs, by key or by the landings it counts (a copy, a clone whose origin is spelt otherwise), is one term of both runs' sums. The recorded shares and the run's must nest over those terms, every residual must hold no one or at least three people, and a repository must carry the same counts over the same landings or in the same window, or the run's shares give way. That closes the rescan minutes, a second or a day later in which no landing entered or left the window, which `--now`'s default, the second of the scan, makes the common case. What remains: a repository whose landings changed between the runs is another term, so one scope shown in both gives the change of its landings, which can be one person's; windows that do not overlap are not compared; and runs answered through another record (another home, a deleted record, or `--egress` pointed elsewhere). The first is the consecutive-runs case, recorded here as the remaining limit for the owner to accept with ADR 0015, with the reason it stays open: closing it means requiring a repository's counts to stay the same across overlapping windows, which withholds every repository that changed for a year after its first share left, or storing shares as sums of weeks, which `ai_assisted@1` does not store, or fixed windows. As first recorded: each run is decided on its own, so the shares of two runs can be subtracted. Two scans of different repositories at the same as-of time give an exact difference at once: a scan of group `ai` shows the group at 4 of 16 with `team` and `solo` withheld, a rescan of `team` alone shows `team` at 1 of 12, and (4 − 1) of (16 − 12) is the solo person's own 3 of 4. One MCP session reads both, since each call reads the latest run and `run-changed` says so. Consecutive runs give a partial difference: their 365-day windows overlap, so one scope's shares differ by the landings that entered and left the window, which can be one person's. Closing both means deciding against the runs already published: keeping, per scope and window, what earlier answers showed, and checking every residual across runs before a share leaves. Fixed windows that do not overlap, such as calendar quarters, would close only the consecutive-runs case, since two scans of different repositories over one quarter still subtract exactly. A cheaper interim step would withhold every scope share in a session once `run-changed` fires; it protects one session, not a model provider that sees several, and it makes one run's answers depend on what the session read before.
  - **One run, two versions**, closed 2026-10-19 ([ADR 0015](0015-shares-are-decided-against-a-record-of-what-left.md), #85): a run's decision is recorded, and every version that answers about the run sends the recorded one, or none when the run's numbers no longer match it (`ai-record-differs`); the first release ships the record, so no later version needs to know what an earlier one would have decided. As first recorded: a run's decision is not stored: every binary that reads the run decides it again. Two versions whose count of people, threshold or order of suppression differ can decide one run differently, and secondary suppression is not monotone in the counts: a lower count can show a sibling the other version withheld, and withhold one it showed. Their answers about the run then subtract to a share both withheld. In the review's group of three repositories, one version shows `r3` and withholds `r2`, the other shows `r2` and withholds `r3`, and the group's share less both is `r1`'s two people's own 2 of 4. No tagged release has shipped any of these rules, so the limit applies to every change of the rule after one does. Storing each run's decision when it is scanned would not close it: a rescan of the same repositories at the same as-of time is a new run, which the new rule decides beside the old run's answers; runs stored before then are still decided by whichever binary reads them; and facts.db is disposable. Closing it needs what the two-runs limit needs (#85): a record of what was sent, kept apart from the run rows, or the floor's rules kept as versions so that a later binary can work out what an earlier one sent, with every new decision checked against it. Either has to start with the first release, or a later version cannot know what that one sent.
  - **Addresses with nothing in common.** The count errs low only where a person's addresses share a name, a local part or a GitHub account, or a rule or a hand-off chain links them. Addresses with nothing in common (another name, another local part, no GitHub account) count as different people until a `.mailmap` line merges them (#86).
  - **The cohort comparisons**, closed 2026-10-15 (#87). In `explain`'s detail (`durable_ai` and `durable_other`, `durable_ai_n`, `durable_other_n`, `reverted_ai`, `reverted_other`) they count a narrower population than the share: changes 90 to 365 days old, and reverted landings, so the detail of a shown share could be one or two people's, and they add up over the scopes as the share does. Of the two ways to close it, counting the people behind each comparison and deciding them with the share's secondary rule, or sending none, the server sends none: a shown share leaves the MCP server with its value, num and den alone and an empty detail, and a caveat says the detail was held back. The rule keeps none of the detail rather than delete the keys known today, so a key a later version adds stays local too (fail closed). The comparisons are stored with the run, and no local page prints them yet. It costs an agent the second half of the metric's question, "how is it holding up against the rest?"; counting them per comparison stays possible if agents need it, at the cost of a second suppression over the same tree.
  - **All or none.** A shown share of 0% or 100% tells each of its people their own tag: no landing was tagged, or every one was.

### Door

Two-way for what is stored: the decision changes only what the MCP server sends. One-way for what has left: answers cannot be recalled, and a later version that decides a stored run differently lets the two be subtracted (one run, two versions, above), so the first release should already keep what #85's ledger needs, and a change of the rule after it should be checked against that. [ADR 0015](0015-shares-are-decided-against-a-record-of-what-left.md) does both: the record ships with the first release, and every new decision is checked against it.

### Revisit if

Answers of runs whose repositories' landings changed in between are read beside each other (the part of the two-runs limit that remains), or the owner decides that the local report applies the rule too. A change of the rule after a release no longer needs this amendment revisited: it decides new runs only, against the record ([ADR 0015](0015-shares-are-decided-against-a-record-of-what-left.md)).
